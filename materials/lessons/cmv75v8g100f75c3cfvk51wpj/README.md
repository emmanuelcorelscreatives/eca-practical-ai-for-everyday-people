# Lesson 93 — Check Ambiguity and Meaning
Emmanuel Corels Academy · Practical AI for Everyday People · Stage 16

This self-authored fictional practice kit accompanies the existing lesson. It contains no private event information and makes no claim of a real translation-tool observation. Spanish examples are authored teaching drafts, not approved translations. Learners generate and label their own outputs and record actual observations.

## Objective
Clarify ambiguous source wording, compare important phrases and fixed details, and treat back-translation as a clue rather than proof. Use a suitably capable reviewer for consequential text.

## Files and route
1. Read `source-notice.md`; identify ambiguity before generating a translation.
2. Use `translation-request.txt`, adapting language variety and intended use explicitly.
3. Complete `review-worksheet.csv`; preserve original source, generated draft and human corrections separately.
4. Compare the deliberately flawed teaching examples in `teaching-examples.md` and consult `answer-guide.md` after your own attempt.
5. Work through `exercise.md`, including the manual fallback and audio usability check.
6. Use `review-and-release-checklist.md` before using a real notice. This fictional kit does not certify a learner's language competence.

No paid or account-specific tool is required. If a translation feature is unavailable, use an ordinary document with side-by-side source, generated wording and corrections. Do not upload real personal information. Keep consequential real text on hold for suitable language-pair and subject review.
