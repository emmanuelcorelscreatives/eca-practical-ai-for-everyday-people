# Lesson 26 — Give the AI a Job, Not an Inflated Identity

Emmanuel Corels Academy • Practical AI for Everyday People

All event data is fictional, self-authored training material. Do not publish a real notice or register anyone. No login, payment, personal data, coding or special assistant product is required. The lesson does not promise that an identity label guarantees expertise.

## Files and evidence boundaries

`source-notes.txt` supplies the seven fixed event facts. `weak-prompt.txt` gives the vague persona request. `weak-draft-example.txt` is an authored illustration containing two deliberate unsupported claims; it is not a captured model response. `improved-prompt.txt` gives a job, audience, source boundary, format and success conditions. `checked-notice.txt` is an authored reference answer, not evidence an assistant generated it. `answer-key.md` explains checks and permissible variation. `check_reference.py` verifies the stored reference's mechanical constraints with ordinary Python 3; using Python is optional, because every check can be completed manually.

## Exercise 1: observe a vague request

Read the seven event facts. If an appropriate assistant is available, paste `weak-prompt.txt` and the seven facts into it. Keep its actual response separately and label it Generated draft — requires review. If none is available, use the authored weak illustration and retain its label. Identify every claim absent from the notes. The stored illustration invents family suitability and refreshments; your actual output may differ. A confident tone is not source evidence.

## Exercise 2: describe checkable work

Read `improved-prompt.txt`. Mark the task, audience, source boundary, constraints and success checks. Submit it with the same facts if you have a suitable assistant. Do not change the dates, times, place, number, instruction or deadline to match a response. Keep supplied evidence, generated text and your independent comparison separately labelled. Asking the assistant to critique its answer gives editing feedback, not independent verification.

## Exercise 3: inspect the actual result

Compare every fact with the original notes. Confirm 17 May 2027; 09:00–11:00; Riverside Community Garden; bring gloves; 20 places; and registration by 12 May 2027. Require no invented address, contacts, benefits or promises. Check for a heading and one body paragraph, 70–90 whitespace-separated words including the heading, and the registration deadline at the end. Missing registration method/contact details should be labelled as missing, never invented. Any response that fails is a draft to edit and recheck, not an acceptable result simply because the prompt was better.

## Exercise 4: manual fallback

In any ordinary text editor, write Task, Source, Constraints and Success checks. Draft from the seven facts yourself, then perform exactly the same comparison. The saved reference has 78 words including its heading, under the stated whitespace-count convention. Different assistants and word processors may count punctuation differently; keep the convention explicit rather than claiming universal count behaviour.

## Transfer the idea

Replace “most accurate researcher” with “List each claim and the supplied passage supporting it; flag missing evidence.” Replace “genius planner” with “Sequence these supplied tasks, preserve fixed deadlines and label missing durations.” A modest plain-language-editor role can guide perspective, but translate it into actions: shorten sentences, use familiar words, preserve names/dates/numbers and flag gaps. Do not add credentials, facts or permissions by assigning a role.

## Completion record

Write your four questions and answers: What work must happen? What information may be used? What must not change? How will I recognise success? Record which supplied facts you checked, which unsupported additions you removed and what remains unknown. Keep this practice local; it is not a real announcement.
