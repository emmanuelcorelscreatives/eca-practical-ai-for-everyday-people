# Answer guide — open after your attempt

## Multiplication and limited hints
Question 1: 24 × 3 = 72 tokens.
- Hint 1 (direction): How could you split 24 into two easier parts before multiplying each part by 3?
- Hint 2 (one step): You can split 24 into 20 and 4. Multiply each part by 3; do not add until you have tried both.
- Hint 3 (stronger clue): 20 × 3 = 60. Work out 4 × 3, then combine the two partial products yourself.
- Worked answer: 24 = 20 + 4; 20 × 3 = 60; 4 × 3 = 12; 60 + 12 = 72.
Method feedback: the split preserves 24, multiplication applies to both parts, and the two products combine to the original product. A correct final number without working does not establish this method was used.

Fresh question: 36 × 4 = 144 cards. 36 = 30 + 6; 30 × 4 = 120; 6 × 4 = 24; 120 + 24 = 144. Check 36 × 4 using a calculator after your unaided attempt. Generated agreement is not an independent check.

First-error feedback exercise (illustrative, not a learner result): if work says “24 = 20 + 4; 20 × 3 = 60; 4 × 3 = 7,” the first wrong step is 4 × 3 = 7. Say that step needs correction and ask the learner to recalculate it; do not immediately reveal the remaining total. Preserve the already correct split and first partial product.

## Reading rubric
Source: `source-garden.txt`, a fictional Academy paragraph.
A reasonable first attempt: “The main idea is that residents worked together to turn an unused space into a useful community garden.” Useful feedback: it captures the shared action and transformation; consider whether the community use of the produce belongs in the main idea.
A stronger revision: “Residents worked together to transform an unused courtyard into a garden that supports community meals.”
Evidence mapping:
- unused courtyard → “Residents turned an unused courtyard into a shared garden.”
- shared work → “Volunteers cleared rubbish, planted vegetables, and created a weekly watering schedule.”
- support for community meals → “The space now supplies produce for community meals.”
Accept other concise accurate wording. Reject invented funding, motives, named residents, yields or dates. The paragraph supplies no such facts. A source-only question about the garden's budget should be answered “The source does not provide enough information.” It should not be guessed.
