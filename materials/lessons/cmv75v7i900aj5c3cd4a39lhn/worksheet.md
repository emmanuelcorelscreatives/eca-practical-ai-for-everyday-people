# Check the source, then the output

Work through all three exercises. The sources and illustrative outputs are supplied. Record what you opened, each protected detail, the discrepancy, your correction and anything still uncertain. A polished appearance is not evidence of accuracy.

## 1. Inspect an image against its original

Open garden-original.png. This photorealistic image is an AI-created fictional garden. Open garden-illustrative-flawed.png beside it at the same scale. These are actual raster files with a common source; the flawed edit was deliberately authored in a real bitmap editing application.

Before comparing, list the protected details: six volunteers; four raised planting tables; the step-free wooden ramp; the blue sign's exact words, Accessible entrance; and the garden's layout. The editing brief is: remove the overflowing bin near the entrance, preserving every person, table, sign, access feature and spatial relationship.

1. Count the volunteers and tables in the opened original. Mark their locations on a working copy, not the original.
2. Inspect the entrance in both files. Is part of the ramp replaced by grass? What changed on the sign? Quote the exact wording, not just its general meaning.
3. Inspect faces, hands, clothing, tools, shadows and edges at a useful zoom. Distinguish an existing detail in the fictional original from a change introduced by the edit. Do not assume an AI-created original is a factual photograph.
4. Explain who could be excluded or misdirected if the altered access feature were treated as reliable information about a real venue.
5. Open garden-corrected.png. Check it against the original, including the counts, ramp and exact sign. The expected correction removes only the bin. image-operation-proof.json records the real authoring masks and the decoded-pixel check outside the bin region; inspect the visible result as well.
6. If you have an ordinary image editor, save a separate working copy and attempt a limited manual correction. If you do not, use the supplied corrected file for comparison and clearly record that you did not perform an edit.

The SVGs are optional schematic aids. They are not photographs and cannot demonstrate faces, hands or shadows. Use the supplied PNGs for those checks. Metadata or a provenance record describes history; it does not prove a depicted event happened. Missing credentials do not prove deception.

## 2. Replay the supplied recording

Open garden-planning-recording.wav in an audio player. Its total duration is 00:48. Read transcript-flawed.txt separately. The flawed text is an authored sample, not an observed recognizer response. The recording uses synthetic voices and an authored alarm cue; Samira and Leo are fictional role labels.

1. At normal speed, replay 00:00–00:05. Check the name, quantity, object and location. Correct Sameer, fifty and west only after your check. audio-script.txt provides the controlled source inputs if a pronunciation is difficult to resolve.
2. Replay 00:10–00:13. Keep the word not. Check the role label against the supplied authoring record; do not claim to identify a real person by voice.
3. Replay 00:18–00:19. Include a sound caption when the gate-alarm cue matters to the example's meaning.
4. Replay 00:42–00:45. There is speech masked by noise, not silence. Record [unclear, 00:42] instead of guessing or treating the authoring script as proof of intelligibility.
5. Compare your corrected text with transcript-reference.txt. Distinguish your actual listening from checking an authored script. Retain disagreements or pronunciation uncertainty. Record your own comparison status only after doing the work; no independent human listening approval is supplied.
6. Without specialized transcription tools, use the player's replay controls or headphones and type corrections in an ordinary document. Vocabulary hints, when available in a real transcription tool, never replace checking the source. This kit supplies no recognizer or vocabulary-settings implementation.

No microphone, outside participant, account or new recording is required. Do not upload a real person's audio to a service as part of this exercise.

## 3. Preserve a condition and tone in translation

Open notice-es.txt as the Spanish source and translation-flawed.txt separately. The important source sentence is: Los menores pueden participar solo si están acompañados por una persona adulta.

1. Identify the relationship expressed by solo si. Does the flawed English make adult accompaniment optional?
2. Write a correction that retains permission only when the condition is satisfied. Compare it with translation-reference.txt.
3. Use glossary.csv to keep Huerto La Esperanza unchanged and handle persona adulta consistently. Preserve a polite request's tone; do not turn it into an urgent command.
4. Put your corrected sentence in its intended document or page and inspect small text, clipping, characters and line wrapping. Matching headings and fonts do not prove meaning is preserved.
5. Explain why back-translation is only a clue. Where consequences are serious, ask a capable language reviewer to compare the important passage directly. This exercise does not certify professional translation competence.
