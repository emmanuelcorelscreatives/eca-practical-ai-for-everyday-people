# Visual choice and verification practice

1. Open attendance.csv, process-source.txt and source-pack.json. State what IS supplied and four things NOT supplied.
2. Choose the relationship for the attendance figures: compare categories, trend over time, ordered process or recognition? Explain why a bar chart fits and a connected line chart would imply unsupported sequence/trend.
3. Check attendance-chart.svg or the practice editor against the opened table: every category once, exactly 48/36/24 people, honest zero baseline, uniform intervals, direct category/value labels, unit and source note. Do not infer unique attendance or future turnout.
4. In the editor, select the hypothetical incorrect example (38 for Repair demonstration). Explain the exact mismatch with 36. Try Export verified slides: it should be held. Restore source, rebuild and verify yourself.
5. Check the four steps word-for-word and in order. Add the hypothetical unsupported “Receive free supplies” step; explain why plausibility is not evidence. Remove it by restoring source. There must be no loop, shortcut or invented branch/time/outcome.
6. Record the difference between opened evidence, an illustration and an assumption. Why does known file origin/edit history not prove the illustrated claim is true?
7. Complete asset-record-template.csv for the kit's original SVG only, using its actual supplied licence. Leave uncertain facts unknown. No permission-cleared event photo is included; do not pretend there is one.
8. Write an equivalent text explanation of the chart including its purpose, all essential categories/values and units. Mark the optional divider decorative or remove it. Shrink the window: can you still read labels without relying on colour?
9. Manual fallback: enter the CSV directly into your own editor; insert a bar chart and compare again. Draw four ordinary shapes with exact source wording and connect only the established sequence.
10. Export the three source-restored slides. Open the ACTUAL exported file and confirm chart, table, diagram, labels, source note and explanatory text survived. Record any clipping/substitution and correct before use. Do not mistake the editing view for export proof.
11. Final decision test: what must viewers understand; what evidence supports it; which visual relationship fits; could placement imply more than the evidence? Choose no visual when nothing adds verified meaning.
