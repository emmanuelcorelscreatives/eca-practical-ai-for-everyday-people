# Manage Context Across Conversations — learner guide
Emmanuel Corels Academy · Practical AI for Everyday People · Stage 6, Lesson 35

## What you will do
Carry a short, user-controlled task brief into a fresh conversation, verify the assistant's restatement, draft a cautious announcement and record its status. This kit is self-authored fictional practice material, not evidence of a real repair event. Nothing in it authorizes publishing, messaging, booking or sending anything externally.

## Files and prerequisites
Open `repair-cafe-source-notes.txt` as the evidence for this fictional exercise. Use `task-brief-blank.txt` to create your own seven-heading brief; compare it with `task-brief-example.txt` after trying. `practice-prompts.txt` provides optional copyable instructions. `verification-and-handoff.md` contains blank checks. `answer-guide.md` is a worked teaching example, not an actual assistant response or completed account action. Save editable copies in a folder you control. Any ordinary document editor or notes app is sufficient. File upload and account memory are optional; interface names and availability vary by account and service. Do not buy or enable a feature for this exercise.

## Practice sequence
1. Read the source notes. Mark the confirmed date, time and location, both repair qualifications and the unknown booking/accessibility information. Do not replace unknowns with guesses.
2. Fill seven headings: Goal, Audience, Relevant Background, Constraints, Required Output, Preserve, Gaps and Current Status. Record decisions rather than your entire chat history. Keep evidence and instructions in separate files.
3. Check sharing permission. Use only these fictional details. Do not add personal participant information or private files.
4. Save the brief. Open a fresh assistant conversation if available. Attach both notes and brief, or paste their text separately with clear labels. A successful upload is not proof of correct understanding.
5. Ask for a restatement and missing-information list before requesting a draft. Compare every substantive point with the brief and source. Correct discrepancies; do not treat the assistant's agreement as independent evidence.
6. State explicitly that the draft must omit unsupported booking and accessibility claims. Ask for one headline and 90–120 words of body text, plus a separate compliance check. Self-review is an inspection aid, not proof.
7. Verify the actual output against source notes, using `verification-and-handoff.md`. Count only body words, excluding headline and compliance check. If a qualification or fact is wrong, repair it before continuing.
8. Move the result into your editor under DRAFT — NOT YET APPROVED. Do not publish. Update the brief status to distinguish what you actually did from work still awaiting organizer approval. If you did not use an assistant, record “manual draft” rather than claiming AI generation.
9. Handoff: give a partner the sources, current brief and draft. Ask them to identify constraints, gaps and next action without reading your conversation history.

## Manual fallback
Without an assistant, write a restatement yourself, then draft from the notes and run the same checks. Without uploads, paste the brief and relevant source text into each new conversation. Without a document editor, plain text or a notes app works. Do not infer that saved memory, projects or history contain every task detail. Account controls for conversation history and remembered information can differ: inspect current controls rather than assuming deleting one removes the other.

## Success criteria
Your brief has all seven headings, source facts and both qualifications stay intact, booking/accessibility remain unknown, the announcement body is 90–120 words, and the saved status accurately says what was checked and what is not approved. The next lesson covers protecting meaning during summarization; this exercise remains about portable context.
