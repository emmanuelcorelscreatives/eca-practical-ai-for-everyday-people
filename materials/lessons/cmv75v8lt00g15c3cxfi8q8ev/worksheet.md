# Build a Search Plan — Lesson 98
Emmanuel Corels Academy · Practical AI for Everyday People

This self-authored fictional Riverside Community Garden exercise teaches planning, not gardening recommendations. No forecast, regulation, plant-care advice or search result has been verified. Do not use it to decide how to water plants.

## Goal
Break the bounded question into focused subquestions and identify suitable source types before accepting a synthesized answer.

Question: How can Riverside Community Garden safely water young tomato plants during a hot, dry week?
Known in this fictional scenario: young tomato plants; hot, dry week; community garden.
Missing: location, relevant dates, temperature, restrictions, soil, available equipment and exact model. These remain gaps; no town, forecast, soil measurement or model is supplied.

## Exercise
1. Copy question-and-gaps.md into any notes application. Keep known details separate from missing details; request essential context or clearly label assumptions.
2. Open blank-search-plan.csv in a spreadsheet or copy its six headings into notes. Fill only subquestion, preferred source type and search terms initially.
3. Split conditions, restrictions, plant needs and equipment into separate questions. Remove irrelevant branches and split rows containing two distinct claims.
4. Match the source to its claim. A weather service addresses forecasts; a local provider/authority addresses restrictions; recognized horticultural guidance addresses plant needs; first-party documentation addresses equipment limits. A product manual does not establish that product is best, and the word official alone is no universal guarantee.
5. Use prompt-and-review.md if an AI search tool is available. Generated output is a draft, never opened evidence. Keep unsupported details in the gaps list.
6. Before opening sources, mark every Evidence found cell Not searched. Record required date/location/plant stage/model scope and remaining gaps.
7. Write sufficiency conditions using stopping-checklist.md. Stop this lesson with a plan, not a gardening answer. Compare your work with worked-search-plan.csv and answer-key.md.

## Manual fallback
Use an ordinary browser and a notes document with four headings: Question, Source type, Search words, Gap. Run focused searches one at a time only when continuing to the next lesson. Copy links or page titles, but never label a claim supported before opening and inspecting its source. A paper notebook remains an optional offline fallback described in the lecture; the supplied digital templates need no account, purchase or AI service.

## Later verification (Lesson 99)
Open candidate pages, identify the creator and date, check geographical and subject scope, locate the relevant passage and look beyond the original page for independent context. Links in generated answers are leads. Agreement among generated summaries is not independent confirmation.

No external commands, logins, datasets, fabricated search results or API keys are needed for this lesson. All files are Academy-authored practice materials, reusable under CC0-1.0.
