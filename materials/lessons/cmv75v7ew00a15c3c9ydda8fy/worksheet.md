# Find what simplification removed

Goal: produce a concise summary that retains conditions needed for faithful, fair and useful decisions. Allow about 25 minutes; repeat at your own pace.

1. Read source-notice.txt first. Write its purpose, intended audience, main rule and limits. Keep the original open as evidence.
2. Read flawed-summary.txt as an authored stand-in for generated material. Mark broad phrases: free, available, accessibility support, food is allowed, same-day bookings are possible.
3. Highlight conditions in the source. Signal words such as only, except, may, subject to and at least help, but also inspect the headings, descriptions and nearby sentences. The east entrance is a condition without a classic exception word.
4. Fill comparison-blank.csv with at least seven rows using the four fixed columns. Quote enough source wording to make each concern traceable. In each final cell, identify a concrete affected person or action rather than saying only “important.” Accessibility may be two rows if helpful.
5. Ask: Who is covered? When? Where? Under what conditions? Who could be affected by an omission? Test whether each omission changes meaning, action, access, obligations, risk or likely decisions.
6. Choose ACCEPT, REVISE, or RETURN TO SOURCE. Explain your choice before opening answers.md. Not every detail needs copying, but every material limit needs a faithful equivalent.
7. Write your own corrected summary, optionally using prompts.txt. Label unknowns. Do not invent capacity, contact details, current availability or acceptance. Preserve the difference between a request and a confirmed booking.
8. Recheck your revision directly against the original. For each source condition, mark preserved or deliberately omitted and explain the reason in preservation-check.csv. Also inspect new claims: where in the source is each supported? Use revised-summary.txt only after attempting your answer.
9. Complete decision-cases.csv. Distinguish changing a summary from returning to a source or seeking missing information before action. More than one step can be appropriate.

Manual fallback: place the notice and summary side by side in an ordinary editor or on paper. Number every source condition. Find its match in the summary; circle missing or weakened conditions and write why each matters. On a phone, finish one source paragraph and its summary match before scrolling. No assistant, upload, citation feature or external action is needed.

Expected checks: registration retained; request versus confirmation retained; 09:00–18:00 and Tuesday–Saturday retained; adult organizer must supervise under-18 events; east entrance and portable hearing-loop request at least 48 hours ahead retained; hot cooking excluded and possible allergy restrictions retained; same-day possibility remains conditional on staff availability; unstated details remain unstated.

Reflection: why can every sentence sound plausible yet the answer mislead? Name one condition that affects a smaller group but remains material. Name one example of unnecessary presentation detail you could omit without affecting a decision; do not pretend that detail appeared in the supplied source.

Completion: comparison rows, an independently rechecked revision, a condition ledger and reasoned decisions. A model's approval of its own text is not a substitute for this comparison.
