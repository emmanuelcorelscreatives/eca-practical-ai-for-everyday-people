# Practice: choose the rules before comparing

## Scenario
Choose a suitable venue for a three-hour community workshop. The fictional options are Harbor Room, Cedar Hall and Junction Studio. We do not yet have venue evidence. Do not rank them or treat unknown information as success or failure.

## 1. State the decision and boundaries
Write the decision sentence. Before researching, agree the event date, one currency, the transport mode, a comparable arrival/departure period and the planned activities. Keep the origin as Central Library entrance, journey one way and booking three hours. These missing planning inputs are deliberately unspecified, not facts to invent.

## 2. Keep pass-or-stop constraints separate
Record availability on the event date, legally permitted capacity of at least 40 and a step-free route to the workshop room. For each fictional venue, status starts Unknown. Mark a condition passed or failed only when opened evidence establishes it. A generic claim of “accessible” does not establish the route or room reached. Attractive extras cannot compensate for a failed constraint. Do not call any option “remaining” until eligibility is established.

## 3. Define the criteria
Open the blank CSV or create a table in a basic document with Criterion, Why it matters, Definition, Unit or rating rule, Evidence needed and Missing-value treatment. Draft total cost, travel time, accessibility beyond the minimum, room suitability and cancellation flexibility. Do not add venue results, scores, weights or a winner. Compare with the worked CSV only after drafting your own definitions. Before assessment, agree explicit requirements for seating layout, presentation space and planned activity space. Meets: all three requirements confirmed met. Partly meets: one or two confirmed met and the others confirmed not met. Does not meet: all three confirmed not met. Unknown: any requirement or its evidence is missing. Never override a failed pass-or-stop constraint.

## 4. Critique without delegating priorities
Optionally copy `review-prompt.txt` with your nonprivate draft into an available assistant. Read its actual suggestions and keep them separate from accepted criteria. The offline page's Generated suggestions tab is a scripted teaching example, not evidence of a model run. Without AI, inspect the same questions yourself: ambiguity, overlap, gaps and inconsistent measurement.

## 5. Decide what to accept
The worked example clarifies whether mandatory equipment is included in total cost; keeps step-free room access as a minimum constraint; and restricts the accessibility criterion to confirmed features beyond that minimum. It accepts public transport practicality because frequency and final walking route matter to this group's agreed scope. It rejects environmental impact for this bounded exercise. These are fictional decision-maker choices, not universal priorities or AI decisions. Record your acceptance/rejection and reason explicitly.

## 6. Check comparability and unknowns
Booking duration, journey origin, mode, period, planned activities and evidence standard must match across options. A full-day quote is not directly comparable with a three-hour quote. Use `evidence-preview.csv` only to see how unknowns will later be preserved. Do not research or fill option values as part of this lesson; that belongs to the next comparison-table step. If later using an estimate, label its method and assumption explicitly.

## 7. Readiness and manual fallback
Check every criterion applies to all eligible options, has one clear meaning, a unit or rating rule, inspectable evidence, a meaningful difference, a distinct role and explicit unknown handling. A basic document or handwritten worksheet can do the same task; no AI is needed. End with fair rules and preserved gaps, not a selected venue.

Submit: decision sentence, constraints with Unknown statuses, your six-column draft, suggestion/decision log, shared-basis checklist and readiness checklist. Do not include private data, invented quotations, scores or rankings.
