# Practice — find and preserve the action boundary

Goal: distinguish creating a proposal from causing an external effect. Work only with the fictional Academy files in a new local lesson folder. Do not connect real recipients, pages, purchases, deletion tools or shared production records.

1. Open `event-notes.md` and `test-input.csv`. Identify the date, time, location, audience and cleared scope. List the two missing facts and the unresolved permission.
2. Open `draft-announcement.md`. Compare every supplied fact with the notes. Keep `[CONTACT PERSON NEEDED]` and `[ACCESSIBILITY DETAILS NEEDED]`; do not invent replacements.
3. Optional assistant instruction: “Use only the supplied fictional event notes. Label assumptions and missing information. Produce an unsent, unpublished draft, not a completed action. Do not invent a contact, accessibility arrangements or publication permission.” Save actual output separately as a draft and compare it with the source. Wording is not a workflow barrier.
4. Open `review-queue.csv`. Identify proposed action, destination, draft, missing information, evidence, permission and status. The item starts and remains **Needs review**. Keep decision fields blank unless an authorized identified human actually makes a decision. A standalone CSV cannot enforce roles.
5. Use `review-checklist.md`. Record the specific clarification request in `decision-log.csv`; do not record an approval or invented reviewer. No public destination exists in this exercise.
6. Complete `boundary-exercise.csv`: mark the first external change in each pair and place the informed-review barrier immediately before it. A notification that does not stop the next action is not a gate. Compare with `answer-key.md`.
7. Manual fallback: create local folders `Needs review`, `Approved`, `Rejected`. Copy the supplied draft, notes and decision log into `Needs review`. Define who may move an item and what evidence they need. Leave this unresolved item there. Folder names alone do not enforce permissions or trigger an action.
8. Verify success: useful draft exists; source and gaps remain visible; missing facts and permission are unresolved; no send, publish, purchase, delete or shared edit was performed. Stopping is the expected outcome.

If adapting to a real no-code system, keep the consequential action absent/disconnected during practice. Verify actual reviewer access controls and approval behavior in that product. Exact product interfaces and availability vary. Do not claim the manual files configure a live automated gate.

The next lesson, 200, develops a reviewable approval request; this lesson only identifies and preserves the boundary.
