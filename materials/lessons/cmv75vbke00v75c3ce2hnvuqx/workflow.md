# Reusable human-review workflow
Trigger: an author-approved source brief and output request are available. Inputs: source brief/version, reusable instruction, output template, intended audience/format and permission boundary. If any mandatory source is unreadable, missing or permission-unclear, STOP; ask the source owner. A model is optional.

Reusable instruction: Draft a welcoming plain-language information sheet using only the opened approved brief. Preserve title, day, time, venue, public contact and booking condition. Label missing details as not provided; do not guess prices, access details or benefits. Exclude internal notes. Drafting does not authorize sharing.

Output template: draft ID/version, status NOT APPROVED FOR SHARING, title, day/time, venue, booking condition, public contact, missing-details note. Keep proposed wording separate from original evidence.

Danger point 1 — source entry: preparer checks required fields are readable, approved and copied from the correct brief; reviewer compares the exact source/version.
Danger point 2 — generated wording: reviewer compares facts character by character, conditions and exceptions by meaning, independently recalculates any numbers and checks permission. A fluent draft is still a draft. Any fail is REVISE or STOP with a reason, never a reluctant tick.
Danger point 3 — final artifact/external action: inspect actual exported/copied bytes and layout, full contact/link text, destination visibility and exact attachment/recipient/content. Approval is for that specific version and action only, with reviewer/date/version/decision recorded.

Manual fallback: create a clearly labeled REVIEW-189 folder containing source, reusable instruction, draft, final artifact, checklist, evidence and decision. No automation is needed. An absent approval feature or unavailable platform is not permission to proceed. Keep sending/publishing disabled.

Change rule: any content, attachment, destination, recipient or date change requires fresh review of the changed version and affected surrounding section. Update paired template/checklist fields together. Reviewer has authority to stop when evidence cannot be checked.

Scale: a private brainstorm may need relevance/readability checks; a public announcement also needs evidence, meaning, permission, destination and action checks. Serious-consequence work may need a suitably qualified reviewer.

Peer trial: ask someone who did not write this workflow to locate the source, perform each observable check, inspect final HTML and identify the stop boundary. Log ambiguous steps and revise the checklist accordingly.
