# Present and hand over your capstone
Keep supporting records visible. Speak plainly and separate facts, examples, unknowns and intentions.

## Six-part presentation
1. Purpose: who needed what useful artifact? What input and permission did you use?
2. Result: show the final local file and its review status. Distinguish an initial draft from corrected quality.
3. Test evidence: describe the complete, changed and missing-information cases. Show source checks, correction records, stop and recovery. Use actual evidence for your own claims.
4. Whole-task time: compare the same task/checks against the manual baseline, including checking, correction, recovery and maintenance. State sample size and unknown categories. Explain whether checked quality justified the whole effort.
5. Limitations: small test set, dependence on source quality, layout inspection, tool/access variation, continued human review and lack of external-action authority.
6. Next improvement: identify the recorded problem, chosen change and expected benefit; state that success needs retesting.

## Worked-example phrasing
“In this authored fictional example, the baseline is 52 minutes and the three totals are 30, 34 and 38. Their mean is 34, an 18-minute difference from the baseline. These are teaching figures, not measured tool performance. Initial drafts need corrections and the incomplete case stops. The proposed improvement is an earlier required-field control. A real retest is still needed.”

For your own work, replace the example with genuine recorded results. If you did not use AI, say so. If a case remained blocked, present that outcome honestly. Do not claim the tool produced the supplied seeded drafts.
