# Lesson 175 — Turn a Service Brief into Clear Copy
Emmanuel Corels Academy | Practical AI for Everyday People | Stage 30

This self-authored fictional ClearDesk kit matches this lesson. It contains no real customers or observed assistant output. Sample drafts and answer keys are teaching examples, never evidence about a business.

## Start
Open approved-brief.md and brief.json. Read every fact and gap. Open claim-review-blank.csv in LibreOffice Calc (UTF-8, comma delimiter); use the supplied completed CSV only after attempting the exercise. Use Writer or any document editor to draft. The lesson method works manually if no assistant is available. If using an assistant, paste prompt-template.txt and only the approved facts; your account interface may differ.

## Exercise
1. Separate approved facts from gaps in a two-column table. Do not fill missing price or timing values.
2. Draft a website description, aiming at 80–100 words without inventing claims. A shorter defensible final paragraph is preferable to unsupported padding.
3. Audit each candidate claim in claim-review-blank.csv against the original brief. Fill Evidence, Status and Action. Use Supported, Unsupported or Needs confirmation; availability needs a current answer.
4. Rewrite the unsupported sample: “Trusted by busy organizations, ClearDesk quickly delivers professional documents that get results. Hundreds of satisfied clients save hours every week.” Explain every removed claim.
5. Keep a final public paragraph separate from an internal missing-information note. Compare to final-public-copy.txt; equivalent fact-preserving wording is acceptable.
6. Before sharing inspect service name, service, deliverables, limits, omitted unsupported claims, and absence of internal notes or clipped/duplicate text. Human approval remains necessary.

## Expected checks
Remote does not establish worldwide availability. Empty evidence stays empty for the 24-hour claim. Never invent a testimonial speaker, results or guarantee. The spreadsheet organizes evidence but proves nothing by itself. The answer key is a teaching reference, not an approval of a real business.
