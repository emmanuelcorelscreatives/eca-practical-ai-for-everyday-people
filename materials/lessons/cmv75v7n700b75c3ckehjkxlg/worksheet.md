# Use a Second AI as a Reviewer Carefully — practice guide

All event and café records are self-authored fictional training material. They are not real notices, model transcripts, organizer confirmations, or completed external actions. Permission: these authored practice files may be copied, adapted and shared under CC0 1.0. No private information is needed. The optional calculator script uses Python 3 standard library only; the full exercise also works with paper and a calculator.

Your outcome: treat agreement and disagreement as leads, then settle important claims with opened evidence. Keep generated material, opened evidence and unresolved information separate.

1. Open `approved-event-note.txt` unchanged. This is the authoritative source only within this fictional exercise. Keep it open while reviewing.
2. Read `assistant-a-study-draft.md` and `assistant-b-study-review.md`. Both are authored simulations of flawed generated material. B deliberately agrees with A on one wrong time. Find that shared error before viewing the answer key.
3. Open `claim-checklist.csv` in a spreadsheet or text editor. For each row, enter exact source lines, your status and correction. Verify the opening, badge count and parking claim yourself, then check the omitted access detail. Do not replace missing confirmation with a claim that parking is unavailable.
4. Reproduce 120 ÷ 8 with a calculator. Then check 14 × 8 and 120 − 112. Record inputs, units and result. The pack calculation assumes one badge per attendee; this assumption is explicitly part of our practice task, not a new fact in the approved note.
5. Write a checked announcement. Preserve 120 places, correct the opening, restore access, remove the parking promise. Keep a separate organizer calculation for badge packs. Compare with `answers.md` only after recording your evidence.
6. Transfer the method to `cafe-practice.md`: compare both authored summaries and verify at least three claims. Record one misleading agreement, one disagreement and one unresolved item in `transfer-checklist.csv`.
7. Explain why two companies, five votes or a confidence percentage do not independently verify a claim. List the source you would open next when evidence is missing.

Optional live practice: use `review-prompts.md` with an available AI service only after checking permission and privacy. Paste only these fictional materials. Keep any actual output separate, labelled with the service and date, and compare it with the unchanged source. No service account or paid plan is required to finish this kit. A live model may flag different things; it is not required to reproduce the authored study responses.

Optional arithmetic helper: from this kit folder run `python3 badge_check.py badge-inputs.json`. Expected results are in the answer key; they are predictions until you actually run it. The script checks integer arithmetic and cannot determine source truth, privacy permission, event readiness or publication approval. No network, installs or file writes are required. You may change a COPY of the inputs to test 121 attendees and 8 badges per pack; expect 16 packs with 7 spare. Invalid or zero pack sizes must exit unsuccessfully.

Manual fallback: list claims; open the original note; compare the time and access wording; redo the arithmetic; record uncertainty; correct supported errors and remove or qualify unresolved claims. This is the same essential checking method without a second AI.

Completion check: your record shows two corrected claims, one unresolved claim removed and one supported omission restored; every decision has a source line or reproduced calculation. For consequential health, legal, financial or safety claims use authoritative sources and documented human oversight as appropriate. This low-risk training note is no substitute for those sources. Lesson 70 checks the final artifact; here we focus on what establishes the claims.
