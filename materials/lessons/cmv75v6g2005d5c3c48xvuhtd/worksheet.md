# Lesson 34 — Work in Sections When the Input Is Too Large

Use the fictional Riverside Community Garden pack supplied here. Files belong to Emmanuel Corels Academy; no private documents or live account access are needed. The six source sections contain the lesson's exact audience, route qualification, advanced workshop and unresolved arrival/toilet details. Dates and venue details are self-authored practice data, not real event claims.

1. Keep `Original_Do_Not_Edit.odt` unchanged. Open a working copy in an ordinary document editor. Its six actual outline headings support navigation. Stable source labels S01–S06/pages 1–12 remain evidence IDs even if your font or application changes pagination.
2. Read S01–S06 and check that each boundary preserves a complete qualification. Do not use a fixed word/page count. A table or appendix may need its own whole section on other documents.
3. Reuse `shared-instruction.txt` without changing its goal. Copy `handoff-note-template.md` and keep it brief.
4. For each section provide three separated blocks: SHARED INSTRUCTION, CHECKED HANDOFF NOTE and CURRENT SOURCE SECTION. Request passages, generated notes, assumptions, missing facts, conflicts and locations. Check every returned claim against the actual source before updating the note.
5. After S01, lock the audience with its evidence. After S02, keep confirmed route separate from unconfirmed toilet. After S03, preserve the advanced-workshop mismatch as open. S04 does not give volunteer arrival; 10:00 is public opening only. S05 protects privacy/draft status; S06 reconciles unresolved issues.
6. Mark all six sections complete only after checking. Then synthesize from checked results plus the note. Compare the entire draft against the originals: route access does not imply all activities are accessible. Keep uncertainty visible.
7. With no assistant, use the heading navigator and stable section files, enter the handoff table yourself, and merge only checked paragraphs in an editor. This is a complete offline alternative, not a degraded exercise.
