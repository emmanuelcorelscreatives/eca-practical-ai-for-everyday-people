# A repeatable account audit

## Prepare
Use only your intended personal account and a new disposable conversation. Read fictional-prompt.txt. Do not open existing private conversations for screenshots. Keep the worksheet local. Never record passwords, email addresses, cookies, full shared URLs or sensitive account identifiers. If a work, school or shared account is managed, consult its administrator rather than changing its controls. The offline exercise remains available.

## Inspect separately
1. Send the harmless prompt only if this account is appropriate. Inspect the generated answer; its privacy claims are not evidence. Record whether the new conversation appears in history. That proves only the visible history observation.
2. Open account settings. Read the explanation of model-improvement choices and record the actual label, state and scope in privacy-audit.csv. If you choose to change it, record what you changed. Do not infer that history disappeared or older training was reversed. Feedback may have separate consequences; read its notice too.
3. Inspect temporary-mode wording before using it. Record the actual retention wording and date. Temporary does not mean zero retention. If a setting is missing, record “Not visible” rather than guessing its value.
4. Distinguish archive and delete. Archive hides a record from the main list while retaining it. If practicing archive, use only the disposable chat, find it in archived chats and restore it if needed for the next task. Before deleting anything, read its confirmation and consider shared access separately.

## Optional sharing and revocation
Sharing is a deliberate external action. Use only the fictional practice conversation. Review the complete proposed snapshot and current access warning before creating a link. Cancel if any content is unsuitable for public access. Never share an existing private conversation for this exercise.

If you choose to create the practice link, open that known link in a signed-out private browser window. Record the actual observation in link-test-log.csv without recording the URL. An error alone does not prove a link is private; networks or sign-in rules can interfere. Return to the signed-in account and locate its shared-link inventory. Remove only the practice link and preserve unrelated entries. Refresh the same signed-out window and record what actually happens. If it still loads, record the failure and investigate; do not claim success from clicking Remove.

Revocation cannot retrieve copies someone already saved, imported or photographed. Close the private window. If you choose to delete the disposable chat, read the current deletion notice first. Never delete unrelated chats or links.

## Missing settings and manual fallback
Labels and availability differ by provider, region, age, plan and account management. If a control cannot be found or changed, record “Unknown — not tested”; consult the account administrator where appropriate. Do not upload first and investigate later. Keep the material local, replace identifying details with placeholders and use offline-fallback.txt. No purchase, sensitive upload or public link is required to learn the three-question distinction.

## Finish
Explain why training off does not necessarily delete history; why temporary does not mean zero retention; and why a link sent to one person can still reach others. Inspect current sources.md references and your actual account notices. Other products may group choices differently. Keep saved history, sharing and improvement conclusions separate. Submit only fictional or carefully redacted coursework if requested.
