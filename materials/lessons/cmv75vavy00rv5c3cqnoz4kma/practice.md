# Guided practice
1. Choose a quiet, safe place and create a NEW practice note. Do not open existing personal notes for a course recording. Check your actual app/keyboard's language and microphone permission. Deny access and type if voice is unavailable or uncomfortable.
2. Read the lamp source card once; identify the object, action, day and time. Dictate its intended sentence normally at a steady pace, then stop. Do not shout. If recording isn't available, type the simulated transcription from `simulated-transcripts.csv` and label it as a simulation.
3. Read the ENTIRE captured sentence. Compare object, action, Friday and 4:30 p.m. with the source. Log what your device actually produced without claiming the provided simulation occurred.
4. Practice the name, quantity and date corrections using the other three rows. To guarantee a visible editing exercise, separately paste their intentionally wrong simulations into a NEW note. Select only the incorrect name/quantity/date, type its intended form, then reread the whole sentence and check spaces and adjacent words. Do not imply you dictated the simulation.
5. For `name`, replace Mara with Maara. For `quantity`, replace fifteen with 50 and compare every digit. For `date`, expand ambiguous 6/7 to 6 July 2027. Do not infer a date from regional conventions: the source card explicitly supplies it.
6. Try two short thoughts separately, checking each before continuing. If noisy/uncertain, stop and type confirmed facts rather than polish an unreliable paragraph. Do not make the environment deliberately unsafe or record others for a noise test.
7. Before using a note in a message, separately inspect its recipient and meaning. Do NOT send these exercises. Accurate captured words do not verify external plans. Friday has no calendar date in this fictional reminder and the shop opening has not been checked; don't schedule a real commitment from it.
8. Record your own experience in `learner-log.csv`. You may leave exact captured text out if private; use only the provided fictional scenarios for shared work. Delete practice notes when finished. Optional accessibility discussion: ask the actual user which speech, language, screen and correction methods suit them; do not infer needs from appearance.

Success: every essential detail matches the intended source after correction; the original unverified capture and manual edits are distinguishable; permission/support/privacy concerns lead to an appropriate fallback. Recognition success is NOT required to pass this exercise.
