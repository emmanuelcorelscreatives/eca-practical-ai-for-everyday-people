# Fictional source notes — staged evidence
These are authored facts for this training exercise. They are not observations from a live spreadsheet or a real organization.

## Initial note A — open during the first audit
P1 means 25 percent of a base quantity of 80 units. The intended stored rate is 0.25. If a cell instead stores 25 and is formatted as a percentage, its percentage display is 2500%. A display alone cannot establish the underlying stored value.

C1 consists of Registration: 80.00 USD and Materials: 40.00 EUR. There is no approved exchange-rate source, rate date, currency-pair direction, rate or fee information. Keep separate subtotals; leave conversion and combined-currency total blank.

D1 has no confirmed date order at this stage. Keep the verified date blank, and ask: “Does 04/05/2026 use day/month/year or month/day/year?”

## Clarification B — open only after recording uncertainties
The fictional N1 source uses a comma for decimals and a period for thousands grouping: 1.234,56 means 1234.56. The separate fictional N2 source uses a period for decimals and comma for grouping: 1,234.56 also means 1234.56. These are source-specific conventions; the workbook locale alone does not prove either import was parsed correctly.

The fictional D1 source confirms day/month/year. Thus 04/05/2026 means 4 May 2026, or 2026-05-04 in year-month-day order. This clarification applies only to D1, not all numeric dates.

The lesson's illustrated workbook uses United Kingdom locale. This kit has no live workbook and makes no claim that a spreadsheet setting was opened or changed.
