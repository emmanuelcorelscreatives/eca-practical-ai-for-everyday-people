1. List all protected workshop facts from `source.txt`.
2. Fill two audience briefs without changing those facts. Explain how audience, relationship, formality, destination and purpose differ.
3. Compare the weak prompt “Translate this into Spanish” with your complete brief. Name at least four missing instructions.
4. Produce or inspect the adult email and teenage phone message. Keep the GENERATED DRAFT label. Record the actual tool used, if any; never claim a generated result was human-reviewed.
5. Explain why the lecture’s “antes del jueves” differs from “by Thursday”. Compare the corrected “a más tardar el jueves”. Then check meaning; every name/contact character; numbers; dates; time range; formatting; required actions; no additions. Record exact evidence and unresolved uncertainty in `review-notes-template.md`.
6. Separately check appropriate address, length, clear action and destination fit. A fluent text can still be wrong for its reader.
7. Optional: play the checked text through an actually available Spanish reader. Record voice availability and observations, without treating playback as meaning validation. If unavailable, use another available reader or ask a capable speaker with permission.
8. Manual fallback: give a human translator source plus full brief, then compare and review. Do not send, post, print or register as part of this exercise.
9. Adapt the transfer brief in `worked-briefs.md`; identify the missing source facts rather than inventing a school event.

Completion requires two distinct briefs, two honestly labeled drafts, a full fact comparison, audience-fit observations, and unresolved review needs documented. It does not require a paid subscription or sending messages.
