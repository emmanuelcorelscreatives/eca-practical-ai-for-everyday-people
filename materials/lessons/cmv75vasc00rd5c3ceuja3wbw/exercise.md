# Fictional community-garden narration exercise

1. Read the exact two-sentence announcement and identify the time, day and requested action. Expected: ten o'clock; this Saturday; bring a reusable cup. Do not invent a calendar date or location.
2. Specify why audio helps this audience. Check that you own or can use every input; use only an authorized general library voice. Record the actual service, voice, plan, terms URL/date, geographic availability and intended testing/publishing use. Unknown means hold, not accepted.
3. Generate the announcement, keeping the original text. Note the actual voice and unchanged initial style controls. Do not clone another person.
4. Listen against the script word by word. Write the exact phrase you heard and action taken. A pronunciation or pacing issue may justify punctuation edits without changing meaning; preserve original and revised text and compare only affected takes if the service supports that.
5. Repeat on headphones and a phone speaker. Ask another person who appears to speak, what the message means and what action to take. Record their actual response, without giving the expected answer first.
6. Add the plain disclosure near the player. Verify final script, audio, corrected captions and visible message agree. Disclosure and consent are separate checks.
7. State a manual fallback: self-record this script in a quiet room; compare every line, fix mistakes, or publish text/captioned silent media instead.
8. Submit your folder for human review. Do not send or publish until deliberately accepted.

## Discussion cards — hypothetical, not observations
- A fluent waveform omits “reusable”: mark the omission and correct the affected passage; do not accept fluent delivery alone.
- A library voice is available for private preview but commercial publishing is unclear: hold publication and check actual terms.
- The result sounds like a colleague and uses their portrait: remove misleading identity cues; do not assume disclosure grants permission.
- Origin metadata disappears in a copied file: keep the human-readable synthetic-narration disclosure visible to the audience.

These cards describe fictional review situations, not failures observed in a supplied recording.
