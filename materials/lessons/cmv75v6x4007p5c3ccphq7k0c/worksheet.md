# Create Your Everyday Prompt Kit
Emmanuel Corels Academy — Practical AI for Everyday People, Stage 8, Lesson 48.

Build a portable six-task library, not a collection of impressive answers. All examples in this kit are fictional/self-created. The sample outputs are AI-generated teaching drafts prepared for this kit, not captured responses from a named assistant, proof of an external action or measured outcomes of repeated model tests. Check them yourself against their supplied inputs. No message is sent by this exercise.

1. Open the six numbered text records in any available notes app or text editor. Keep an ordinary local copy outside a chat service.
2. For each record, complete every bracketed field before using its instruction. Use only information you own or may use. Keep private identifiers out.
3. Try the normal, missing-information and awkward inputs in `test-cases.json` with an assistant of your choice, or apply the rules manually. Save the exact output and record what actually happened in `test-log.csv`. Blank observations mean **not run**, never PASS.
4. Compare each protected fact with the supplied source. An assistant's approval of its own response is not independent verification. Use the record's checklist and manual fallback.
5. When a case fails, retain it, change the smallest relevant instruction and rerun that case. Explain one improvement and its observed effect. Do not claim this kit's hypothetical failure history happened in your own assistant.
6. Complete `submission-checklist.md`, then make a plain-text backup and reopen it. Storage features do not establish correctness.

## Evidence labels
**Supplied input** is the fictional source you can inspect. **Generated sample output** is the draft in each record. **Checked item** has a recorded source comparison or deterministic check. A sample's expected behavior is not a measured model test result.

The rewriting sample follows the lesson: Saturday 24 October 2026, 09:00, east gate, gloves, water, cancellation in heavy rain. Its teaching revision is version 1.1 with the authored example review date 13 October 2026; that historical scenario date is not a claim about today's preparation or an actual run. Learners record their own current date.

## Completion
All six records need a use case, required inputs, reusable instruction, fictional example, labelled generated draft, acceptance checks, three observed test records, limitation, fallback, version and review date. Keep uncertainty visible; stop instead of guessing when the source cannot support an output.
