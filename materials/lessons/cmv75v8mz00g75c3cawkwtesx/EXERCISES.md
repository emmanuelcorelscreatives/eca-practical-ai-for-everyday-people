# Research practice
1. Read generated-summary.txt. Separate the generated answer from opened evidence. List four claims to check; a citation is a lead, not proof.
2. Open index.html and the Visitor Hours page. Find Saturday. Record publisher, update date, location/scope, exact passage and remaining gap in a copy of worksheet.csv. Explain why 8 p.m. is unsupported for this exercise.
3. Open Booking and Entry. Preserve both the general admission recommendation and the school-group exception. The family group's size is unknown. Do not turn a school-group rule into a rule for every group of 15. Seek clarification before deciding whether another group policy applies.
4. Compare the Family Day Out Guide's East Gate with the current Accessibility Guide. Record the exact North Gate passage and its date and site. The contact link is an offline exercise note, not a message form or a claim that anyone was contacted.
5. Read both lines of Temporary Notices. State the affected area and dates while retaining the main garden qualification. Do not infer a whole-attraction closure.
6. Classify every source in source-catalog.csv as first-party, derivative, sponsored or direct evidence where justified. Categories can overlap. First-party material does not automatically prove neutrality; independent reporting is useful for comparisons and criticism.
7. Trace the derivative guide to the archived 2024 schedule. Its old 8 p.m. statement is not a current-hours source. Identify the affiliate disclosure; do not conclude that every sentence is false solely because of it.
8. Write a bounded finding, attaching each checked claim to its supporting passage and retaining group size and special-event changes as unresolved.

Manual fallback: use regular browser search for real research, then open destinations and read around matches. Saved illustrative query strings (not executed searches): `"Riverside Science Garden" "North Gate" site:visit.riverside.example` and `"Riverside Science Garden" booking -tickets`. The .example domain is reserved and fictional. Quotation marks, site filters, minus terms and date filters help discovery but do not establish truth. In this offline exercise, use the source index instead of expecting a fictional domain to resolve.

Self-check: use answer-key.csv only after your attempt. A correct answer preserves all qualifiers and gaps; a polished unsupported answer is incomplete. Optional: run `python3 verify.py` in this directory. This validates kit consistency, not external facts or a learner's completed work.
