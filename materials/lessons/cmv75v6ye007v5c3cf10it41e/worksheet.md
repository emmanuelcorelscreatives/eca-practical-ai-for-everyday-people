# Source-checking exercise

1. Open the fixed **generated-material example**, not as evidence but as claims to investigate.
2. Copy each exact claim into the five-column worksheet: Exact claim; Opened source; Supporting passage; Result; Correction or note.
3. Open the cited source itself. Search a distinctive phrase, then inspect surrounding passages and relevant headings. Zero text matches alone are not proof that a proposition is false.
4. Compare the date, number, unit, place and time period precisely. A topical page or a citation badge is not exact support.
5. Record Supported, Unsupported or Unresolved. In this exercise Unsupported means the cited page does not support the claim as written; it is not automatically universal proof of falsity. Use Unresolved where unavailable evidence leaves the proposition unsettled.
6. Leave the alleged speaker blank if no attribution is demonstrated. Do not invent a correction.
7. Write a short corrected summary using only demonstrated details; retain the correction trail. Check consequences before deciding how much verification is enough.
