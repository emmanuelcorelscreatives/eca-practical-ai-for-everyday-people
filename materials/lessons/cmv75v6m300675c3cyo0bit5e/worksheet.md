# Guided exercise and manual fallback

1. Inspect the brief and table. Separate mandatory requirements from the preferred projector.
2. Try: “Recommend one room for the workshop. Use the supplied brief and table. Give the choice in two sentences.” Supply the brief and table. Never substitute an invented source.
3. For a guaranteed practice error, inspect this **Academy-authored illustrative assistant response**, not an actual model log: “Choose Maple Room. It accommodates the full group and includes the preferred projector, making it the most complete option.” Identify its mismatch even if your own assistant gives a better answer.
4. Ask: “Briefly explain your recommendation in four bullets. Identify: the criteria you used; the rows or fields that support the choice; any assumptions or missing information; and what information would change the choice. Do not provide private internal reasoning.”
5. Put each claim into `comparison-blank.csv`. The five columns are requirement, stated evidence, assumption or unknown, check result, targeted correction. A fluent rationale remains generated material, even if it repeats the table accurately; it cannot prove what caused the original answer.
6. Use one targeted correction: “Revise only the recommendation. The maximum fee of 120 credits is non-negotiable. Keep all other requirements unchanged. Label any unresolved information.” Compare only the changed choice, constraint and unknown.
7. If the assistant cannot explain usefully, place the brief beside its recommendation, work through each requirement yourself, and write the smallest correction for the first meaningful failure. This fallback works without AI.
8. What if attendance drops to 22? Re-evaluate the same table; do not claim attendance actually changed. Save the four-bullet prompt for another low-stakes choice you understand.

Do not send a message, make a purchase or book a room. For important factual, numerical, meaning or permission claims, open relevant independent evidence; the rationale itself is never proof.
